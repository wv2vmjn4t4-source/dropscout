How not to lose your wallet chasing airdrops

Most "airdrops" that land in your DMs are phishing. Five rules to keep in mind.

Nobody needs your seed phrase

No real project, bot or "support agent" will ever ask for your 12 or 24 words. If they do, it is a scam.

Use a separate airdrop wallet

Keep one address just for airdrops and hold only enough on it for gas.

Check the domain

Take links only from the project's official X or Discord. Phishing sites are often one letter off.

Read what you sign

Requests like setApprovalForAll and unlimited approvals give away access to all your tokens.

Revoke old approvals

Once a month, review your token approvals in a revoke tool and remove the ones you no longer need.

Scams going around right now

"Claim now" DMs

Real projects announce claims on their own X and site. Treehouse, for example, states it never sends claim links by DM.

Fake claim fees

A real claim costs only network gas. A site that asks you to pay a "fee" or "tax" to unlock tokens is stealing.

Random tokens in your wallet

Unknown tokens with a website in their name are bait. Don't trade or approve them, just hide them.

Search ads for claim pages

Fake claim pages buy the top ad slot. Type the address yourself or use the link from the project's verified X.

Fake support in Discord

Admins never DM first. "Support" asking you to verify your wallet on a link is draining it.

Don't get filtered as a sybil

Filters are strict

Before its ZRO airdrop, LayerZero removed 803,273 wallets, 59% of all applicants. Treehouse also cuts flagged wallets.

Don't chain-fund wallets

Funding many wallets from one address, in the same amounts, at the same minute, is the clearest sybil signal.

Act like a real user

Use the product on different days, with varied amounts. Ten organic weeks beat one day of 500 swaps.

Keep history

Old wallets with ENS names, NFTs and DeFi history score better than fresh empty ones.

Hear about new airdrops first

One weekly digest of vetted airdrops plus snapshot reminders. No spam.
